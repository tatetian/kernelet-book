# One scheduler, hierarchical, preemptive

There is one scheduler and it is preemptive; there is no vCPU thread and no guest scheduler beneath a host scheduler. Alternative B's task groups are adopted as the *shape* of the kernelet's `CpuBudget`: each kernelet is a group in the host's injected scheduler with a weight, a quota, an affinity set, and the run time of every task in it charged to it on every scheduling event; a kernelet reorders its own threads through `set_sched_attr`, bounded by its group. What is not adopted is letting the kernelet pick the task: the host's scheduler picks within the group by the attributes the kernelet set, so the host always knows who holds what, and a kernelet cannot deschedule its own lock holder in a way the host cannot see.

The tick enforces two budgets, both keyed by `(KerneletId, ThreadRef)`: three consecutive ticks with preemption disabled or a host lock held (3 ms at the tree's 1 kHz), after which the kernelet is killed on the tick's return path; and, until OSTD preempts in kernel mode, 100 ms of consecutive kernel-mode ticks at lock depth zero, a fairness hole rather than a safety one. Kernel preemption itself, a switch on the kernel-mode interrupt-return path when the preemption count is zero, is an OSTD change ([§5.1](../../implementation/ostd-changes.md)); with it, a task holding a host lock is exempt from quota throttling until its depth returns to zero, so a tenant cannot park a host mutex behind its own exhausted quota.
