# When a kernelet dies mid-conversation

Alternative B's crash rules for `RRef` are adopted as written, because they are exactly what the host table makes cheap. Buffers the dead kernelet owns are released and their charge refunded to the dead kernelet's account, which is being reclaimed anyway. Buffers in flight to it (queued on its worker's queue, owner already the dead kernelet) are the same case. A live kernelet's `RRef`s are untouched, because the owner of each is the live kernelet and nothing inside a buffer can name a dead one's; a connection to the dead kernelet is reset, and the peer's next `send` fails with the connection error vsock defines. There are no borrowers in this design, only owners, so Alternative B's "mark invalid until the borrowers release" state does not arise: a buffer is always owned by exactly one kernelet or by the pool.
